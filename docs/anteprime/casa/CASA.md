# La casa nuova — the new home, its pages and the tools

The plan Imad approved on 2026-11-12 ("va bene"), written down so that the work can go on without him and with any model. His words are in Italian. Read `CLAUDE.md` first (his decisions), then this; the cat is in `docs/anteprime/gatto/DOT.md`.

## 1. What he asked for

- "allora va bene lasciare la bento ui, puoi prendere ispirazione da https://www.ericwu.me/": a bento home, inspired by that site's ideas (filters in the bar, a hero with a sentence and link buttons, project tiles with pictures, a working mini-tool inside a tile), never its code or its look; no italic type.
- "metti anche la mia heatmap per tipo github"; "voglio che ci sia un riquadro tools con tanti strumenti da poter usare [...] ognuna di queste singole cose deve essere fatta bene bene bene, curata nei minimi dettagli e ogni singola cosa deve essere aesthetic con animazioni e cura"; "vorrei avere una sezione progetti, con tutti i progetti che ho fatto".
- The interactive element that is not inside a tile is now **Dot**, the cat, who lives over the tiles.
- "io sposterei cubo tra i progetti ma come immagine dei progetti mettere l'animazione del cubo; al posto di palestina, metti tipo world [...] dove parlo del sudan della bosnia di tutti i conflitti."
- Why all this: "non capisco cosa aggiunge nella vita delle persone [...] vorrei renderlo utile". The tools are what a visitor takes away; the rest says who he is.
- He works on a phone and judges on a phone. He said: work alone, no questions unless vital. **Replacing the real home at `/` needs his OK**; everything else goes online at its own address, not indexed, when the checks pass.

**Dot on the home**: starts when the intro has finished. First time in a browser session: the opening (keys, box, "salam, sono Dot."); after that, Dot is simply around and the box stands in its place. The look is **`manifesto`**: Imad chose it on 2026-11-12 ("manifesto"). Load only that look; no look switch on the home. Dot's layer sits under every sheet or panel that opens (the Quran reader above all) and above the tiles. The dock's height goes to `bottomInset`. A filter or any layout change calls `DOT.refresh()`. The sound button also mutes Dot. `data-dot="quiet"` is strict: Dot never chooses that tile's edge or an edge right under it, leaves at once if dropped there, and never plays there.

**Live tiles, how they behave**

- **Tools**: one field ("Incolla un link", 16 px type so the iPhone does not zoom) and the code beside it, drawn by `QRCore`: at every change the modules redraw with a short ripple; empty field = a faint code of `https://imad.chat` with "Esempio" / "Resta sul tuo telefono.". "Salva" gives a PNG (share sheet on phones when available). Under it one quiet line, "Presto: QR Studio · Palette · Convertitore", until their pages exist. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Heatmap**: 53 weeks by 7 days on a canvas, sized to fit the tile (about 5 px squares on a phone); the squares light up in a wave when the tile first comes into view; a finger or the mouse moving over it scrubs: a line above says the day and its count ("mar 4 nov · 3 contributi"); the total of the year; a small `GitHub ↗` link to his profile. Colours: the site's warm gold on the tile's ink, five levels, readable in light and dark. No data: empty squares and "si aggiorna tra poco". Never sample numbers.
- **Sto imparando**: "Three.js Journey", the last one or two commit titles of his `threejs-journey` repo with how long ago, and a small wireframe solid turning slowly, drawn with lines on a 2D canvas. Opens the repo. No data: "si aggiorna tra poco".
- **Progetti**: the cube animation, "Progetti", opens `progetti`.

**Data contracts (route handlers in the Next app; the static pages only read them)**

- `GET /api/github/contributi` → `{ ok: true, user, total, from, to, days: [{ d: "YYYY-MM-DD", n: count, l: 0..4 }], updated }`, oldest day first; on any failure `{ ok: false }` (never a made-up value).
- `GET /api/github/progetti` → `{ ok: true, repos: [{ name, description, language, url, homepage, pushedAt, stars, fork }], learning: { repo, url, pushedAt, commits: [{ message, date }] } | null, updated }`; public repositories only, the owner's own first, forks last; on failure `{ ok: false }`.
- Both cached for hours on the server, with a short timeout towards GitHub. A `GITHUB_TOKEN` is optional. From this workspace GitHub cannot be reached at all: the handlers are checked for real only after a deploy (`https://imadchat.vercel.app/api/github/...`).

**The QR engine** lives in `docs/anteprime/casa/tools/qr/`: `qr-core.js` (`window.QRCore`: payloads, `encode`, `render` to a canvas with shapes, colours, a picture in the middle and a draw-in progress, `svg`, `verify` that decodes the drawn pixels again) on top of two vendored libraries: `qrcode-generator` by Kazuhiko Arase (MIT) to build the matrix and `jsQR` (Apache-2.0) to read it back. Sources and licences in `docs/fonti.md`.

**Pages in this round** (under the preview address for now, built so that moving them to the root later is one setting in `build.mjs`): `anteprima/progetti`, `anteprima/mondo` (and one page per file in `content/mondo/`), `anteprima/su-di-me`. `tools` and `tools/qr` come with QR Studio in the next round.

## 2c. Dot in the intro, the passage to the box, the hint (asked on 2026-11-12; built after the home, as previews)

His words: "voglio che tu faccia capire che puoi interagire con il gatto, trascinarlo ecc, in una maniera subdola semplice furba, senza invadere troppo lo schermo; trova anche un modo (con un'animazione) per passare dall'animazione iniziale all'animazione del cubo che si piega su se stesso in una scatola; poi vorrei che il gatto fosse implementato anche nella intro in qualche maniera". He chose the look first: **Manifesto**.

Common rules:

- Everything is drawn with the real skeleton and the Manifesto look (`dot-rig.js`, `look-manifesto.js`): never a second drawing of the cat.
- The live home at `/` and the intro he loves stay exactly as they are. The new pieces are a version of the intro called `gatto` (the default on the new home's preview; `?v=stella` shows today's intro to compare) and they run only where Dot's scripts are loaded.
- Like the rest of the intro: one time `t` drives everything and `__ni.seek(ms)` draws any instant. With reduced motion none of this plays.
- Dot is never drawn in the letter-windows, never near the picture of the Kaʿba or the flag.

**A. Dot in the intro: one gag. The cat is the reason for the name** ("chat" is cat). At most half a second longer than today.

1. While `imad chatraoui` is typed, nothing of Dot is seen.
2. In the pause after the last letter Dot comes up from behind the end of the line, behind `raoui`: the peek pose, cut at the top of the letters as if the line were a ledge (on the dark screen its black body under the line is not seen): ears, fez, eyes, two paws resting on the letters. It looks at the caret, then at the visitor; a short hold.
3. It pushes the letters off, as cats do with things on a table: first `i`, slowly, with a look at the visitor; then `u`, `o`, `a`, `r` in one quick sweep of the paw. Each letter tips forward around its foot, falls with gravity, turning a little, and fades below the line. No letter just disappears. The caret goes back to the end of `chat`.
4. Dot looks at what is left, `imad chat`, blinks slowly and sinks back behind the line, ears last.
5. The star falls and becomes the dot, as today.
6. When the nine characters have settled in the 3x3, Dot jumps from below into the centre square (a dark arc, the tail last); the square dips under its weight and the gold dot blinks once. That is the key that becomes its box.

**B. The passage from the intro to the box: one shot, no cut.**

1. The 3x3 has settled and Dot is inside the centre key.
2. A press runs over the nine squares in reading order, about 30 ms apart. Each dips and, coming back up, splits in two: its cap (the letter, now a cream key cap: the very picture the stage draws) lifts toward the viewer with a soft shadow; its base stays behind.
3. The nine bases fly out and grow into the nine tiles (today's landing). The nine caps keep their 3x3, shrink to the size of the stage's keys and travel together along one arc to Dot's ledge above the hero, the rows arriving one after another, the bottom row last, with a small settle.
4. On arrival the stage takes over on the same frame: `DOT.stage` gives the intro the nine key rectangles for a given place and a painter for one cap, so both draw the same thing; the intro's canvas lets go, and Dot's opening goes on from "keys formed" (fold by columns, then by rows, the key drops, becomes the box, the lid, two eyes, Dot comes out, "salam, sono Dot."). Meanwhile the wordmark is typed in the hero, as today.
5. The passage adds about one second before the page is free; the page can be scrolled as soon as the tiles have landed. Later in the same session: no passage and no opening (the tiles land as today, Dot is simply around).

**C. The hint that Dot can be touched: no words, small, on Dot only.**

1. On a computer the pointer is a hand over Dot (`grab`, then `grabbing`).
2. Dot notices a finger or a pointer that comes near: ears up, head and pupils turn to it, the pupils widen; a finger scrolling past close to it gets a quick bat of the paw.
3. **The ghost fingertip, once.** The first time in a session, about four seconds after "salam, sono Dot." has gone, if the visitor has not touched Dot yet: a small translucent round fingertip (about 24 px, like the touch dot of a screen recording) fades in on Dot's back, presses (a ring), lifts Dot a little along an arc (Dot hangs long and unimpressed), holds half a second, puts it down (Dot lands and shakes), lets go (a ring) and fades. Dot looks at where it went, then at the visitor. About 2.6 s in all. A real touch at any moment cancels it. Never again once the visitor has dragged Dot (remembered on the device); in two sessions at most.
4. **Dot knocks.** If still nobody has touched it and the page has been still for a while with Dot in view: Dot walks to the edge, lifts a paw toward the viewer and knocks twice on the inside of the screen (a small ring spreads on the glass at each knock; silent), tilts its head and waits with big pupils; then it gives up and loafs. Twice in a session at most, never while the visitor is scrolling or typing.
5. The first real touch gets the warmest answer (a happy hop and a purr): it must be clear that the cat responds.

No bubble, no word, nothing away from Dot, everything gone in seconds.

**D. Manifesto, the chosen look.** Keep its character (graphic, flat poster cat, long tapered tail, tall ears, ring eyes); fix only what is wrong: a head turned aside must read as a cat's profile (a small muzzle with the nose at its tip, the near ear in front of the fez), never a ball with one eye; the two forelegs of the sitting cat are two legs with two paws, not one column; darker whiskers outside the head on the light theme; the hind legs hang a little apart when it hangs; pink pads when it slides and the paws drawn over the things it holds (as Adesivo did); the eyes keep one clear highlight. Then `look-adesivo.js` and `look-pelo.js` leave the pages and the repo, and the look switch leaves the `/gatto` preview.

**E. Where Dot stays on the home (decided 2026-11-12 night, after seeing the new home online).** Seen on a phone: Dot sitting on a tile's top edge covers the bottom of the tile above it (the "Progetti" title, the Tools line, the name on Su di me).

1. Dot may cover pictures and drawings: the globe, the photo, the cube, the squares of the heatmap, bare glass. Dot never covers words or controls (titles, lines, numbers, labels, buttons, fields, links, chips) and nothing of the Corano. The home passes the whole list as `keep`: no short list, no exceptions.
2. Three perches are always good, so Dot is still in view most of the time: **the hero ledge** (its home, with the box), **the dock** (the top of the filter pill: it is fixed to the screen, so Dot rides along while the visitor reads) and **the 3x3 logo of the footer** (its own keys). Tile edges stay perches wherever Dot's real outline passes the `keep` test (for example over the heatmap's squares, or beside a title).
3. Dot follows the visitor. When it has been out of view for about four seconds and the page is still, it comes to a good perch in view; if there is none, to the dock. It never appears from nowhere: it comes up from behind the dock's edge (ears, eyes, then a hop on) and leaves the same way; between two perches that are both in view it walks and jumps as usual.
4. On the dock Dot is calm: it sits, loafs, sleeps, and watches what scrolls under it (the pupils follow); when a filter is tapped it looks down at it. It stays above the pill: never over a filter's word, the tail on top or behind, and a tap on a filter always reaches the filter.
5. Measured on a phone (390x844 and 360x780, scripted reading and scrolling): Dot in view at least 85% of the time; 0 frames over a word, a control, the Corano or the quiet tile, travelling included.

**F. Small things of the same round.**

- The intro with the cat lives in its own file, a copy: `docs/anteprime/intro/intro-gatto.js` (versions `gatto`, `stella`, `cursore`), loaded only by the new home. `intro.js` and `intro.css`, the live home's, are not touched: `public/home` stays byte for byte as it is.
- The Projects list leaves out the profile repository (the one named like the user).

## 2d. La casa più bella (decisa il 2026-11-12 notte)

Imad, before going to sleep: "vorrei che iniziassi a lavorare da ora sulla bento ui, che mi facessi un cambiamento che rendessi tutto più bello, puoi fare tu (con le tue idee)". The home online had felt "senza carattere, piatto, banale". Four explorers looked at four lanes (materials and light, type and composition, motion and touch, living tiles and colour); this is what the art director chose from them. The target pictures and the prototype (override files loaded on the unmodified page) are in this session's scratch folder `bella-scr/direzione/` (`insieme.css`, `insieme.js`, `toppe-insieme.json` for the markup, `pagine-insieme.*` for the pages, `insieme.cjs` the loader, `finale/` the before/after sheets). They are the target, not code to paste.

**The idea, in one sentence.** The home is Torino's real sky right now, the same sky on which the intro typed `imad.chat`: only the windows onto that sky are dark, everything that informs is glass, the Corano is paper, one real sun lights every edge, one gold (the dot, the star, the greeting) marks what is alive, the machine speaks in Geist Mono and people in Manrope, the tiles move like the keys of a cube, and Dot, black with his red fez, lives on their edges.

### A. One of each

1. **One sky.** The page behind the tiles is Torino's sky at this hour, top to bottom, computed from the same colours as the hero. In the dark theme it is deep (at night almost the intro's own black: rgb 5,7,16 at the top), so the hero and the globe read as lit windows; at night the intro's own stars stay behind the tiles, faint. In the light theme it is the pale sky of the same hour. Nothing on the page drifts.
2. **One material rule, the same in both themes.** Dark are only the windows onto the sky: the hero (the real sky now) and Le città (the globe). Everything that informs is glass: Nel mondo, Progetti, Sto imparando, Tools, Heatmap, Saluti (graphite in the dark theme, veiled white in the light one). The Corano is paper. Su di me is a photo. Heatmap and Sto imparando leave the ink material; Saluti loses its dark-theme night.
3. **One light.** Every surface gets the same sun (`--sun-ang`, `--shx`, `--shy` from `vivo.js`): a bright rim on the side of the real sun, fainter opposite; a three-layer shadow cast away from it. In the dark theme the tiles now cast shadows too.
4. **One accent: gold**, the dot of `imad.chat`, the star, the greeting. Clay stops being an accent (only warnings). Colours that belong to things stay with those things and are never UI colour: Dot's red fez and green eyes, the Kukko pastels (only inside the cube), the Torino photo.
5. **Two voices.** Manrope for names, sentences and big numbers (people). Geist Mono, uppercase, small, for what the machine knows: the hero's dateline (`TORINO · 23:30 · NOTTE`), the right-hand meta of a tile (`VERSETTO DI OGGI`, `CODICE QR`, `GITHUB ↗`), the commit's time (`IERI`), the months of the heatmap, the "presto" line, the section headers of the inner pages. Tile names stay Manrope (`Corano`, `Le città, adesso`, `Su di me`): an all-mono label set was tried and read as a developer template, colder on the Corano.
6. **One axis, one padding, one header row.** Tile padding `--p` 18 px on phones (16 under 375 px, 20 from 700 px); the text edge (wordmark, dateline, "Ciao", labels) is `--axis` = gutter + `--p` = 34 px on a 390 phone. Every tile's label sits at the same place, the chevron centred on the label line. Inner radii are concentric: `--r-c` = `--r` − `--p` = 14 px.
7. **One motion language**: things are physical objects. They press under a resting finger and spring back; after the intro their drawings wake along the diagonals of the 3x3; a filter moves them in rows like a cube's layers; the dock's pill is one liquid shape; a page grows out of its tile where the browser can. Tiles never move on scroll. The Corano never moves.

### B. Tokens (new ones are added; every old token keeps its name)

Values are written in `style.css` (and `pagina.css` for the pages) for both dark blocks (`@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) :root:not([data-theme="light"])` and `:root[data-theme="dark"]`). Tokens marked *computed* are written by `app.js` every minute and on a theme change; the CSS value is only the starting value.

- **Page sky** (computed): `--cielo-alto`, `--cielo-mezzo`, `--cielo-basso` from the hero's `zen`, `mid`, and `low` = LCh mix(mid, hor, .62), with `tone(colour, L, C)` in OKLab; `day = clamp((alt + 4) / 14)`, `dusk = clamp(1 − |alt + 1| / 10)`, `night = clamp((−alt − 6) / 8)` (alt = the sun's altitude over Torino). Dark: alto L .13 + .12·day, C .022 + .055·day; mezzo L .15 + .12·day + .025·dusk, C .026 + .045·day + .015·dusk; basso L .175 + .06·dusk + .11·day, C .03 + .05·dusk + .02·day. Light: alto L .962, C .016 + .012·day; mezzo L .948, C .022 + .01·day + .008·dusk; basso L .93 − .006·dusk, C .03 + .03·dusk. `--cielo-bagliore` = the sky's glow colour at alpha .04 + .3·dusk·(1 − .6·day) (dark) or .06 + .34·dusk (light); `--cielo-gx` = `--sky-gx`. `--stelle` = night in the dark theme, 0 in the light one. `--stelle-tex` = a 220 px tile of 26 seeded stars (seed 1111) drawn once at 2x. Both `theme-color` metas take `--cielo-alto`. Starting values: `#eef0f8 / #e8e9f5 / #ece6f0`.
- **Glass**: `--vetro-base` (computed): dark rgba(tone(mid, .265 + .04·day, .018 + .012·day), .9 − .12·day) (rgb 39,41,50 at night), light rgba(tone(mid, .985, .006), .62). `--vetro` dark = radial-gradient(130% 58% at 50% −8%, rgba(255,255,255,.055), transparent 70%), linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(255,255,255,.05), rgba(255,255,255,.012)), linear-gradient(var(--vetro-base), var(--vetro-base)); light = linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(255,255,255,.3), transparent), linear-gradient(var(--vetro-base), var(--vetro-base)). `--tile-solid` #26282f dark, #f6f6fb light (the ground of a glass tile while it travels). No live blur on any tile.
- **Night**: `--notte` = radial-gradient(120% 78% at 50% −4%, #27336f 0%, #151d47 50%, #0b1031 100%) (tiles; `--night-mat` stays for the sheets).
- **Paper, dark theme only**: `--paper-mat` radial-gradient(120% 70% at 50% −12%, rgba(255,228,180,.12), transparent 62%), linear-gradient(180deg, #332e26, #27231d) (paper in shade, no longer brown); `--paper` #2b2620; `--paper-ink-2` rgba(242,234,216,.68). Light paper unchanged.
- **Light** (per material): rim `--filo-1/2/3` and shadow tint `--ombra` with `--o1/2/3`. Shadow of every tile: `0 0 0 .5px rgb(var(--ombra) / calc(var(--o1) * .45)), 0 1px 2px rgb(var(--ombra) / var(--o1)), var(--shx) var(--shy) 26px −12px rgb(var(--ombra) / var(--o2)), calc(var(--shx) * 2.4) calc(var(--shy) * 2.8) 72px −30px rgb(var(--ombra) / var(--o3))`. ### A. What the visitor sees and does, from the last tile to the end of the page

1. **The passage.** After the Saluti tile the page sky goes on (56 px, 72 from 700 px), then deepens, row by row, into the real sky: no edge, no title. In the light theme the pale page passes through a blue hour, never through grey. The stars come out one at a time, the brightest first, as at dusk; then the Moon and the planets; the low profile of the Alps rises from below with the compass. The sky stays still on the screen while this happens (a stage pinned for 52% of a screen of scrolling), and turns 26° toward what is worth looking at.
2. **The sky at rest.** One screen: the sky of this minute from the horizon to the zenith (on phones), the Alps and the hill of Torino, the compass along the horizon, under it the hero's own dateline (`TORINO · 23:41 · NOTTE`), then the line "Scegli un saluto: diventa una stella di questo cielo." and the first row of greetings. Dot comes to sit on the horizon. The first time, one quiet line in the sky says "Trascina per guardarti intorno · tocca una stella".
3. **The sky is an instrument.** A sideways drag turns it (360°, with inertia); a vertical one scrolls the page. A tap on a star, a planet, the Moon or the Sun opens its card. A finger that rests draws the constellation figures around it, which then fade.
4. **The ground with the greetings.** Scrolling on, the ground rises: the sixteen greetings of `D.greetings`, each in its own script with the name of its language, as a calm field of words on the earth of Torino. Under them, one honest line.
5. **The flight.** A tap on a word: it dips, lifts and turns gold, its letters gather into a point of light inside it, the point swells and rises along a bowed curve with a short trail, the page rises with it until the whole sky is on the screen, the light lands with a small overshoot, a bloom and a thin ring, and the label slides out of the star: the word and the time (`salam 23:41`). Dot's eyes follow it. About 2.4 s.
6. **Afterwards.** The star has a true place (right ascension and declination, fixed when it was born): it rises and sets with the real stars. Coming back hours or days later the sky opens toward it, wherever the sky has carried it; if it is below the horizon a line says when it rises again. One star per visitor: another greeting sends a new light into the same star.
7. **The end of the page** is the ground of Torino: the footer (the nine keys, the Torino line, the credits) stands on the same dark earth, in both themes.

### C. The sky (the engine: `cielo.js`)

- **Real, computed on the phone** for Torino (45.0703 N, 7.6869 E, 240 m) every 15 s and when the tab comes back. Sun, Moon and Mercury to Saturn from astronomy-engine 2.1.19 (vendored, MIT), with refraction near the horizon; 1,630 stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (public domain, from `lib/astro/catalog.ts`) taken from J2000 to the equator of date and then by local sidereal time to altitude and azimuth. Checked against `lib/astro`: stars within 8.5e-5°, the Moon 3.2e-5°, the Sun 1.9e-3° (`lib/astro` uses a simpler solar formula).
- **Projection**: stereographic, centred on the horizon: the horizon is a straight line, the zenith at the top of the stage on phones (`k = max(yh/2, W/(4·tan 30°))`).
- **Colours**: the hero's own (`--sky-zen/-mid/-rose/-hor`, written by `app.js`), deepened at night toward rgb(3, 5, 14) by .32/.28/.18/.10; a glow on the Sun's side.
- **Stars**: radius .45 + .27·(5.3 − m) px (more below m 1.5); colour from B−V, blended 45 to 92% with the intro's warm white (255, 250, 238); dimmed and warmed near the horizon (.25 magnitudes per air mass); the faintest star shown follows the Sun's altitude (none above −4°, magnitude .4 at −6°, 5.2 at −18°) and loses .9 under a full high Moon; each breathes with its own period (2.6 to 5.8 s, amplitude .13 to .30), like the intro's; halos below magnitude 2.3; Italian names (from `stars.json`) only on stars brighter than 1.6: 500 10 px, white .42. Planets do not breathe and carry their names; by day only Venus (.55) and Jupiter (.22).
- **The Moon** at its place with its phase, the lit limb toward the Sun, earthshine, the maria by its real axis (drawn 8.5 to 13 px: larger than true, as on every chart). **The Sun**: a 9 px disc and a 120 px halo. **The Milky Way**: a faint seeded model of the galactic plane (1,900 soft spots; said to be a model in `docs/fonti.md`), only with the Sun below −11°.
- **By day the sky is honest**: blue, the Sun, no stars. The dateline then gains a fourth piece, computed: `PRIME STELLE 17:33` (the Sun at −6° this evening), while the Sun is above −4°. The visitor's star is the one star seen by day.
- **A shooting star**, the only thing in this sky that is not computed: only at night (Sun below −12°), the first 25 to 60 s after the sky is at rest, then one every 2 to 5 minutes; 620 to 880 ms, high (46 to 72°), faint; never during a touch, a flight or with a card open. Nothing says it is real.
- **The horizon**: seven real peaks at their apparent height from Piazza Castello (Monte Rosa, Superga, Colle della Maddalena, Monviso, Rocciamelone, Uja di Ciamarella, Gran Paradiso; heights ×1.15), a drawn ridge between them (indicative, said so), haze and a warm city glow at night; the earth is the horizon's tint, very dark (OKLab L .105 at night to .20 by day), written on `:root` as `--cielo-terra`. The compass, 5 px under the horizon: ticks every 5° (3/5/7 px), degrees every 15° in Geist Mono 10 px, `N NE E SE S SO O NO` in Manrope 12 px (700 for the four cardinals), brighter toward the centre. Four peak names, 10 px, white .36. No towers, no domes.
- **The passage** is a function of the scroll only: `P = (H − top)/(H + pin)` (top: the section's top edge on the screen; pin: 52% of a screen; P is .66 when the stage pins and 1 when the sky is at rest). While the canvas top is on the screen, each of 16 gradient stops takes the colour the fixed page sky has at that point of the screen and goes to the real sky along an OKLCh path (lightness straight, the short way round the hue, chroma lifted halfway by .052 in the light theme, .012 in the dark one); the band is .92 of a screen tall in the light theme, .62 in the dark one, and once the stage pins it slides off the top (gone at P ≈ .92). Stars appear at P = .34 + .065·(m + 1.5), over .14; the Moon .36 to .6, planets .4 to .7; the horizon rises from P .5 to .96; the words of the earth fade in from .86 to 1. Below the band the canvas is opaque: the intro's stars of the page sky never double with the real ones.
- **Arriving**, the sky looks toward the visitor's star if it is up, else the Moon, else the Sun, else the brightest planet, else the south.

### I. Motion

The sky is one object that turns (inertia: decay 330 ms; glides: ease in-out, 520 to 900 ms); lights are physical (they gather, rise along curves, overshoot a little, settle); words slide out of what they name; nothing fades in place of a movement. Never moving: the horizon line once at rest, the Arabic and the verse, the dateline, the field of greetings (a word moves only when it is chosen), the ground, the footer. The page moves by itself only in two cases, both stopped by any touch: the scroll down from "Lascia un saluto" and the rise with the light during a flight.

### J. Budgets

One sky canvas (device pixel ratio capped at 2) plus the flight canvas for the 2.4 s of a flight. The engine (116 KB) and the data (about 125 KB) are loaded only when the section comes within 120% of the screen. Drawing only while the stage is in view, the tab visible, no sheet open, the intro gone: at rest at most 30 times a second, 60 while something moves. No layout read inside a frame loop (one rectangle per scroll event). Measured in headless Chromium on a shared machine (not a phone): drawing 1.4 ms on average while turning (p95 4.7) at CPU x1, 4.05 ms (p95 11.4, max 15) with the CPU slowed four times; the builders keep the average under 6 ms and every frame under 20 ms at x4. If drawing stays above 9 ms for 30 frames the Milky Way is dropped for the visit. 60 frames a second on a phone is plausible, not proven: nothing here runs Safari or a real phone.

### A. The theme button (`comune/tema.js`, loaded first in every page's `<head>`)

- `#tema`, `class="iconbtn tema"`, before `#sound` in `#bar`, the same 44 px glass as the sound button (36 px seen), `data-dot-keep`. Icon 20 px: a ring r 6.4 px, stroke 1.6, with a fill clipped by a rect whose width springs to 0 (light), .5 (follows the phone) or 1 (dark) of the disc (spring damping .7, 420 ms). Never a crescent, never a moon, never a sun with rays.
- **States**: `auto` (default, follows the phone), `chiaro`, `scuro`. **Every tap flips what is on screen**: the new theme is the opposite of the one shown; if it equals the phone's, the state becomes `auto` (the key is removed), otherwise it is saved forced. So on a dark phone: auto (dark) → chiaro → auto (dark) → chiaro; nothing ever "does nothing". Saved in `localStorage["imad.tema"]` (try/catch; private mode just does not remember). `?theme=light|dark` applies to that page only and is never saved.
- **Before the first paint**: `tema.js` is a small synchronous script (≤ 4 KB, no DOM wait) placed right after `<meta charset>` and the viewport meta, before the inline script and every stylesheet. It sets `html[data-theme]` when forced (the CSS already has both guards everywhere: checked, every `prefers-color-scheme` rule in casa/home, comune, mondo is guarded; a check enforces it, see G), rewrites the two `theme-color` metas to the forced colour (`--cielo-alto` start values #eef0f8 / #070810) and **wraps `window.matchMedia` for exactly the two queries `(prefers-color-scheme: dark)` and `(prefers-color-scheme: light)`**: the returned object answers `matches` from the effective theme and fires `change` (to `onchange` and to `addEventListener`/`addListener` listeners) when the button or the phone changes it; every other query goes to the real `matchMedia` untouched. This serves casa.js (`DOT.setDark`), dot-brain.js, intro-gatto.js, cubo.js without editing them; app.js, vivi.js, vivo.js and pagina.js already observe `data-theme`. No rewriting of stylesheet media text.
- `window.TEMA = { get() → "auto"|"chiaro"|"scuro", dark() → bool, phoneDark() → bool, set(state, {from}), flip({from}), on(fn), off(fn), vt() → the running ViewTransition or null }`; `fn({ state, dark })`.
- **The change**: where `document.startViewTransition` exists and motion is allowed, the new theme is revealed by a circle that grows from the button's centre to the farthest corner, `clip-path: circle()` on `::view-transition-new(root)`, 560 ms on `--molla-piana` (no overshoot on a clip); `ready` and `finished` rejections are caught (no console error); skipped while the intro plays. A change while the circle runs skips it and applies at once after it (no circles on top of each other). During a view transition every tap goes to `<html>`: on the home (`filo.js`) a tap skips the circle and its click reaches the button under the finger, and a finger that lands on a thread grabs it (its `pointerdown` is redone on the thread's hit area). The Corano tile is never crossed by the circle's edge: while `html.tema-vt` it has its own `view-transition-name` and switches at once (`filo.css`). Elsewhere and with reduced motion: instant. The HUD: a 30 px glass pill slides out from under the button to its left (translateX 8 → 0, 320 ms `--molla`, stays 1.2 s, leaves 200 ms) with "Chiaro", "Scuro" or "Come il telefono"; `aria-live="polite"`. aria-label: "Tema: scuro, come il telefono. Tocca per il chiaro" (and the matching three).

### B. The yarn (`home/filo.js`, `home/filo.css`)

- **Two ropes**, ids `tema` and `suono`, knotted under the centre of their buttons (knot y = button bottom − 2 px). Drawn on one `canvas.filo` inside `#home`, `pointer-events: none`, z 3 (under Dot's layers, over the page sky), sized to the ropes plus 150 px, DPR min(devicePixelRatio, 2).
- **Physics** (Verlet, fixed step H = 1/240 s, at most 8 steps per frame): N = 16 segments, total rest length 52 px (3.25 px each), gravity 1700 px/s², damping .9963 per step (90% gone in about 2.5 s), 12 constraint passes, bend stiffness .035, stretch .45 while pulled (the wool gives). Sway: the page's scroll acceleration × .045 (clamped ±1100 px/s²) and a pointer passing within 30 px (its velocity × .25 on the nearest 3 points). The loop sleeps when every point moved less than .02 px for 30 steps; nothing runs while still.
- **The look**: two-ply twisted wool, 2.15 px thick (down to 1.6 when stretched), twist step 2.5 px, the colours of Dot's own ball of yarn (`look-manifesto.js`, the `yarn` prop): ply #e3765c, second ply #b9533f, highlight #f29a82 on the sun side (`--sun-ang`), knot #a8473a, the same in both themes (warm, next to the clay of the old pill and Dot's fez, equal to neither), a knot 4.5 px at the button, the end frayed in three 3 px fibres. No outline.
- **The pull (lamp cord)**: the hit area is `div.filo__presa` around the rope only (13 px padding, at least 30 px wide, `touch-action: none`, `data-dot-keep`, hidden while `html.ni-on`); the grab takes the nearest point within 30 px. Pulled 26 px past taut and below the knot, it **clicks**: the button's toggle runs (`#sound.click()` for the sound, `TEMA.flip({from: button})` for the theme), the button hops in its socket (vy −140 px/s, socket spring k 900, ζ .55), a 6 ms tick through dot-sound if sound is on; the cord can click only once per grab. Released: the stretch relaxes by .97 per step (about 140 ms), the fling is capped at 1600 px/s, it swings and settles in about 1 s.
- **Arrival**: the threads drop straight from the knots (every point starts on the knot, still, and no point ever rises above the one before it; the rest length grows 0 → 52 px over 620 ms on `--molla-piana`); the sound thread starts 140 ms after the theme thread; at 42% of the drop each gets a small sideways push, the same side, not mirrored (end swing about 3 to 4 px), settled by about 1 s; 700 ms after `IMAD.whenFree`, or 8.6 s after it on Dot's first-visit opening (so they never compete with the box). With `?shot=1`: still and hanging. With reduced motion: still and hanging, a tap on the thread toggles at once.
- **The tear-off (Dot's gag)**: a weight hung on the rope (`FILO.hang`) pulls it; when the tension stays over 1.32 × the weight's still tension for 90 ms (`handle.tug(f)` adds force, for the phone where the knot is only 69 px above the ledge), the button **is torn off**: it falls with its thread, spinning 2.2 to 3.6 rad/s, lands on the hero's top edge (rounded corners included) in about 195 ms, rolls and rests; 0.95 s after it is still it hops (vy −320) and a spring (k 120, ζ .62) carries it back up to its socket, settling with a 1.04 pop about 2.1 s after the tear. **The toggle does not happen on a tear**: the cat never changes the visitor's own choices (a cat that mutes the page or turns it dark reads as a bug). While away the button is `pointer-events: none` and `aria-disabled` (about 2 s); its socket shows a faint ring (`--hair`).
- **`window.FILO`** (page coordinates in CSS px, y down) is the contract with Dot: see H.

### C. The cursor (`home/cursore.js`, `home/cursore.css`; computers only)

- Only with `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; never on touch; off with `forced-colors: active` and `prefers-contrast: more` (the system pointer stays).

## 4. The tools

Rules for all of them: everything is computed on the visitor's phone, nothing is uploaded, no account, no ads, no tracking; each tool has its own address to send to a friend; it works one-handed on a phone first, then on a desktop; the result can be saved or copied in one tap; every control answers at once, with motion that explains (things draw themselves, colours flow, numbers roll), never decoration; dark and light; reachable by keyboard; honest about limits. One tool is finished before the next starts: "meglio tre fatti benissimo che venti a metà".

1. **QR Studio** (`/tools/qr`). What goes in: a link, plain text, Wi-Fi (name, password, type), a contact (vCard), an e-mail, a phone number. What it looks like: the shape of the modules (squares, dots, rounded, joined), the shape of the three corner eyes, colours (one, or a gradient) and background (also transparent), **a picture in the middle** (upload, with a clean margin around it and automatic high error correction so it still scans), a frame with a caption ("Scansiona"). The code redraws itself while he types. Checks that matter: enough contrast, quiet zone, the code is decoded again in the page to prove it scans before saving. Out: PNG (large), SVG, copy to clipboard, share sheet on phones.
2. **Palette** (`/tools/palette`). Five colours; tap or space to shuffle, lock the ones to keep; harmonies (analogous, complementary, triad, shades of one); **from a photo** (the main colours, computed on the device); every colour editable (hue, lightness, chroma in a perceptual space so steps look even); names of the nearest common colours; a contrast check for text on each pair; a preview on a small mock interface; export as CSS variables, Tailwind config, an image, a link that reopens the same palette.
3. **Convertitore** (`/tools/converti`). Units (length, weight, temperature, area, volume, speed, data), colours (HEX, RGB, HSL, OKLCH), number bases, time zones (the five cities of the site first), images (PNG, JPEG, WebP; resize and compress; say plainly when a format cannot be read on that phone).
